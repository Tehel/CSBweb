# 3D2D1D

3D2D1D is a puzzle dungeon built on CSBWin. Its data set is `3d2d1d`
(`static/gamefiles/manifest.json`), and its files are `static/gamefiles/3D2D1D/dungeon.dat` and
`graphics.dat`. `static/gamefiles/3D2D1D_CSBwin.zip` is the dungeon as it was distributed:
`config.txt`, the same `dungeon.dat`, `GRAPHICS.DAT`, and a `CSBwin.exe` built on
2019-07-02.

What the dungeon holds and how its three puzzles work is in
`static/gamefiles/3D2D1D/ANALYSIS.md`, and a full solution in `WALKTHROUGH.md` beside it.

The port loads the dungeon but cannot play it as designed. It needs two CSBWin features
that our `doc/CSBwin-src/` has but never turns on: **vertical moves**, bound to two keys,
and **gravity**. Porting them is `DEBT.md` X5.

## Two versions of CSBWin

`doc/CSBwin-src/` is the 15.x source (`APPVERSION "15.7"` in `CSBlinux.cpp:24`,
`"Version 15.9"` in `CSBwin.rc:313`). The `CSBwin.exe` in the zip is newer: it reports
`CSB for Windows/Linux Version 2019.07.02`, and its FileVersion is `2019.7.2.1`.

In our source, both 3D flags are hardwired to `false` and nothing ever assigns them
(`doc/CSBwin-src/data.cpp:240-241`):

```cpp
bool ThreeDMovements = false; //For 3DMaze
bool GravityGame = false;     //For 3DMaze
```

The 2019 exe sets both from the dungeon, in `ReadExtendedFeatures`. The only evidence for
that is its disassembly (`objdump -d -M intel CSBwin.exe`, 32-bit PE). Its version of the
`efb.extendedFlags` block (`doc/CSBwin-src/SaveGame.cpp:396-431` in ours) stores two more
bits than our `EXTENDEDFLAGS` enum has (`doc/CSBwin-src/CSB.h:491-504`, which stops at
`0x80`):

| Bit | Global | Set at | Read at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `0x400` | `ThreeDMovements` (`ds:0x50ef02`) | `0x470e18` | `0x437bfe`, the `0x4a00`/`0x4900` test of `TAG0196da` |
| `0x200` | `GravityGame` (`ds:0x50ef03`) | `0x470e0a` | `0x42f20f`, which does `gravityMove = true; nextGravityMoveTime = d.Time + 2` (`CSBCode.cpp:8166-8170` in ours) |
| `0x100` | `SupressDSAWarningOption` (`ds:0x50e7c8`) | `0x470dd1` | not examined. 3D2D1D does not set it |

The two globals are identified by what reads them, and by their layout: they sit side by
side after `AutoEnlarge` (`ds:0x50ef00`), in the order `data.cpp:239-241` declares them.
Both lie past the end of the exe's `.data` (`0x4eb000 + 0x3c00`), in zero-filled memory,
so their initial value is `false`, as in our source.

CSBWin 20230608's source has all three: `SupressDSAWarning` 0x100, `GravityGame` 0x200 and
`ThreeDMovements` 0x400 (`doc/CSBwin-src/CSB.h:501-503`), read into
`SupressDSAWarningOption`, `bGravityGame` and `bThreeDMovements`
(`SaveGame.cpp:415-416`, `:427-430`). The port reads them into `ctx.rules`
(`readRulesOptions`, `src/data/CsbwinExtendedFeatures.ts`), and 3D2D1D's `extendedFlags`
is 0x6b0, both 3D bits. Nothing in the port plays them yet (`DEBT.md` X5).

**Source policy.** `doc/CSBwin-src/` stays the specification. A newer CSBWin source, if
one turns up, is used only for targeted inspections of what ours lacks, such as the two
bits above, and never replaces it.

## The dungeon's header

`dungeon.dat` opens with an Extended Features block (15052 bytes in all):

| Field | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `version` | `'B'` | six-byte SCROLL records |
| `flags` | `0xab` | `LevelDSAInfoPresent`, `0x02` (not in our enum; `dm-windows` sets it too), `IndirectText`, `ExpandedPortraits`, `BigActuators` |
| `extendedFlags` | `0x6b0` | `ExtendedWallDecorations`, `SequencedTimers`, `ExtendedTimers`, **`0x200` gravity, `0x400` 3D moves** |
| `numDSA` | 6 | descriptions in the file: "1-D Controller", "S0/S1/S2/S3 Copies Teleporter A to B immediately", "S0 Causes pulse at A and B" |
| `cellFlagArraySize` | 3975 | |

No other dungeon in `static/gamefiles/` sets bit `0x200` or `0x400`. `dm-windows` has
`extendedFlags = 0xf0`. `dm-atari` and `csb` have no Extended Features block. So the
dungeon is the gate: DM and CSB never get vertical moves or gravity, and the port needs
no per-dataset switch to keep them from being used as a cheat.

The maps (`npx tsx tools/peek-dungeon.ts --file static/gamefiles/3D2D1D/dungeon.dat
--graphics static/gamefiles/3D2D1D/graphics.dat levels`) are map 0 at 32x32, with the party
starting at (3,2), maps 1-7 at 15x15, map 8 at 32x32 and map 9 at 32x8. The shafts
between levels are **fake pits**: open (bit 3) with bit 0 set, for example flag `0x49` at
map 1 (1,1). `MoveObject` drops an object or the party only through an open pit whose bit
0 is clear (`doc/CSBwin-src/Code11f52.cpp:3050-3055`), so the party stands on these
without falling.

## The two keys

`config.txt` binds them:

```
scan 1 00000c 490000  ; '-' Move down level
scan 1 00000d 4a0000  ; '+' Move up level
```

Every number in `config.txt` is hexadecimal. `0x0c` and `0x0d` are PC scancodes, the keys
right of `0` on the main row (`-` and `=`/`+` on a US layout). Here is how a press reaches
the party:

1. `UIM_KEYDOWN` translates the scancode through the `SCAN` entries of the `KEYXLATE`
   table (`doc/CSBwin-src/CSBUI.cpp:1141-1160`, filled by `ReadConfigFile` at
   `:796-802`), and queues Atari key `0x490000` or `0x4a0000`.
2. `TAG0196da` shifts a key with an empty low word down to `0x4900` or `0x4a00`
   (`doc/CSBwin-src/CSBCode.cpp:8525`). Neither is in any `KeyXlate` table. Only
   **`if (ThreeDMovements)`** does it queue button `0x1124` for `0x4900` (Down) or
   `0x1125` for `0x4a00` (Up) (`:8575-8595`).
3. `_HandleMouseEvents` sends `0x1124` and `0x1125` to `MoveParty` with the other moves
   (`doc/CSBwin-src/Mouse.cpp:1695-1709`).

## A vertical move

`MoveParty` (`doc/CSBwin-src/CSBCode.cpp:8031`) maps `0x1124` to relative direction 4 and
`0x1125` to direction 5 (`:8091-8093`).

- **Where it goes.** `UpdateXY` (`:4661-4671`) keeps the party's x and y and asks
  `IncrementLevel` (`:4774`) for level +1 (direction 4, down) or level -1 (direction 5,
  up). `IncrementLevel` also shifts x and y by the two levels' offsets. Past the first or
  last level, the target is stone (`:8262-8268`).
- **The pit rule** (`:8269-8284`). Down takes the flags of the square the party stands
  on. Up takes the flags of the target square on the level above. The move is allowed
  only if that square is `roomPIT`, whether open or closed, real or fake. Anything else
  counts as stone.
- **A monster on the target** refuses the move with no sound and no damage
  (`:8286-8291`).
- **The move itself** is `TraverseStairway(direction-4, false)` (`:8307`): 0 goes down and
  1 goes up (`:7830-7834`). `false` keeps the party's facing. `TraverseStairway` runs the
  party-move filter as a `PM_STAIRWAY` move.
- **Stone** is a wall bump (`:8309-8377`). With champions in the party, the two whose
  side faces `(direction + partyFacing + 2) & 3` and the next side take 1 damage each
  (`DamageCharacter(…, 1, 24, 2)`), and sound 18 plays if either was hurt.
- **Before any of that**, the move has already run the party-move filter as
  `PM_ATTEMPTMOVE`, with `absDirection` 4 or 5 and the target type `roomSTONE`
  (`:8191-8208`), and charged the stamina of an ordinary step (`:8175-8190`, `:8216-8222`).

`MoveParty`'s own comment labels `absdir` 4 as up and 5 as down (`:8148-8149`), the reverse of
what `UpdateXY` does. The code is what counts: direction 4 goes down.

## Gravity

With `GravityGame` set, every `MoveParty` call sets `gravityMove = true` and
`nextGravityMoveTime = d.Time + 2` (`doc/CSBwin-src/CSBCode.cpp:8166-8170`). Any move
does, even one that is refused.

**The move history.** A move that is not itself a gravity move records its axis and sign
in `moveHistory` (`:8128-8164`, `doc/CSBwin-src/data.cpp:246`, initially `{0,1,2,0}`):

- The axis is 0 for east-west, 1 for north-south and 2 for vertical.
- `moveHistory[0..2]` order the three axes from most to least recently used.
- Bit `axis` of `moveHistory[3]` is set when the last move along that axis went the
  positive way: east, south, or down.

**Each gravity step.** `_HandleMouseEvents` starts with this whenever `gravityMove` is
set (`doc/CSBwin-src/Mouse.cpp:1482-1491`). It empties the mouse queue, so the player's
clicks and keys are thrown away for as long as gravity runs, and asks
`GravityMove` (`:1259-1370`) for the next move:

- **Too soon.** Before `nextGravityMoveTime`, it returns nothing and the queue stays
  empty.
- **Choosing the step.** It tries the axes from the least recently used,
  `moveHistory[2]`, to the most recently used, `moveHistory[0]`. Each axis is tried
  **one square in the direction the party last moved along it**, if that square is on the
  map:
  - Horizontally, the target must be open floor or a pit.
  - Down needs the party's own square to be a pit. Up needs the target square to be a
    pit.
  - A monster on the target rules that axis out.
- **The step.** The first axis that passes becomes a queue entry: button 3-6 relative to
  the party's facing, or `0x1125`/`0x1124`. It is handled like a player's move, and
  through `MoveParty` it sets up the next gravity step.
- **Stopping.** When no axis passes, `gravityMove` goes back to `false` and input is
  accepted again.

The result is that the party keeps sliding and dropping along the directions it last
took, until nothing moves.

In our source, `SaveGame` does not save `gravityMove`, `nextGravityMoveTime` or
`moveHistory`. The two flags need no saving: they come from the Extended Features block,
which a save carries as its block 0.

## What the port has

- `EF_EXTENDED_FLAGS` (`src/data/CsbwinExtendedFeatures.ts`) stops at `0x80`, like
  our source. `readRulesOptions` reads only the rules flags.
- `KEYSCAN` (`src/renderer/ui/Keyboard.ts`) has no `0x4900` or `0x4a00`.
- `MovementSystem` (`src/engine/MovementSystem.ts`) ports only relative directions 0-3.
  `Game.handleMouseEvents` (`src/engine/Game.ts`) does not handle `0x1124` or `0x1125`,
  and has no gravity hook.
- The six DSAs all sit on map 9 and use four commands only: MESSAGE, OVERRIDE,
  COPYTELEPORTER and GOSUB. The dungeon has no special locations, so it has no party-move
  filter. All four are in `DsaSystem`. A hand probe of the engine on the real files ran
  the corridor's chain as CSBWin does: a pad, the controller's copies, a lever with its
  ornament rotation, the teleport. It also ran map 6's generator, which made a group of
  Screamers. No tool proves this yet.
- **A 3D2D1D game saves**, its six DSAs with it, and loads back (`tools/dump-dsa-save.ts`).
- `tools/solve-3d2d1d.ts` models the two missing features outside the engine. It computes
  `static/gamefiles/3D2D1D/WALKTHROUGH.md`, and replays it.
