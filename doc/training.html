Training in Dungeon Master

How to gain Fighter, Ninja, Priest and Wizard levels as fast as the game allows: what earns experience, what only looks like it does, and the multipliers that matter more than either.

Read from Paul R. Stevens' CSBWin C++ source · Numbers computed from the Atari graphics.dat and dungeon.dat · Three observations from play, marked as such

The short version: the weight of what you throw earns nothing, and where you train matters more than what you do. A Ninja practising at the bottom of the dungeon while a monster attacks earns 24 times what the same Ninja earns alone near the top.

Three factors on every gain

Every experience gain goes through one routine, which scales it three ways before adding it to the skill.

×½Fighter and Ninja skills, when no monster has attacked the party for 150 ticks (about 40 seconds).
×1–6Every skill, by the dungeon level the party is on.
×2Every sub-skill, when a monster attacked in the last 25 ticks (about 6 seconds). Priest and Wizard spells included.

A class's level is the mastery of its base skill, and every sub-skill's experience is added to that base as well. Mastery goes up by one each time the experience, halved at every step, is still at least 500. So level m needs 500 × 2m−2. Each level costs twice the last, so the multipliers matter more the higher a champion gets.

Quick wins

  1. Take the long stairs down

    Two staircases, starting on levels 6 and 7, go straight down to the bottom levels. Once you can reach them, training at the bottom is a free win: ×2 or ×3 becomes ×5 or ×6, for every skill. (From play.)

  2. Hit a closed door, not the air

    KICK, BASH, HACK, BERZERK, SWING and CHOP go at the door when the square in front holds a closed door. The game checks whether the door can be broken only afterwards, and either way the attack counts as a hit, not a miss. So it earns its full experience, with the busy time fixed at 6.

    A door that can't be broken can be kicked for ever. A breakable one gives way after a hard enough blow, and then the training is over. KICK earns 13 at a door against 6 in the air. BERZERK earns 40, busy for 6 ticks instead of 30.

  3. Throw at the wall in front of you

    A thrown object checks for a wall before it leaves your square, so it drops back at your feet. It's the same reason a fireball cast at an adjacent wall explodes on the party. Each throw costs two clicks, picking it up and throwing it, and you never walk after it.

  4. Let the priest feed the fighters

    YA potions are the best priest training, and they give back exactly what kicking and throwing use up: stamina. The priest casts YA and hands it over, the kicker drinks it, and the empty flask goes back for the next cast. Mana goes in; two classes' experience comes out.

  5. Sleep to get mana back

    Mana comes back every 64 ticks when awake and every 16 asleep: four times as fast.

  6. Keep a harmless monster busy on you

    Next to a monster that keeps attacking, every sub-skill earns double. Fighter and Ninja skills also lose their ×½, so they earn ×4. Only the monster's own attack counts; a missile arriving from elsewhere does not.

  7. Train on a Black Flame, on level 13

    The best monster for it. A Black Flame never moves, and it is immaterial: no sword, kick or club can hurt it. A blow at it still counts as a hit, not a miss, so it earns full experience, and the flame is never used up. Its own attack keeps the ×2 on, and level 13 gives ×6: the highest multipliers, with no limit.

    Two of level 13's flames stand alone in a corridor, far from the others. Each appears when the party steps on the square just east of it, and the party trains from there, facing west: the flame at x7,y1 from x8,y1, along the top of the level, and the flame at x2,y18 from x3,y18, in the bottom-left corner.

    Its blow is fire, which a fire shield takes off. Two priests casting FUL BRO NETA, at MON if they can, keep the damage at little or nothing. (From play.) Only the two champions in front can reach the flame, so put the fighter and the ninja there and the priests behind. Leave the Dispell spell out of it.

    With the Vorpal Blade, it trains the Wizard too. The Vorpal Blade is the one blade that hurts the flame. Its DISRUPT trains Air, a Wizard skill, with no mana, and against anything that isn't immaterial it is a miss. A blow that hurts also earns a second gain, which grows with the damage. In the end the flame dies, but the one at x2,y18 comes back fast. Its generator rests 704 ticks (about three minutes) against 2,816 for the one at x7,y1. Then step off the pad and back on.

What doesn't help

Weight: it earns nothing when thrown. Punching the air: it counts as a miss, and a miss earns half. Skill increasers: the engine has one, but no actuator in Dungeon Master's dungeon uses it. Gear: the Firestaff, Pendant Feral, Ekkhard Cross, Moonstone, Gem of Ages and Sceptre of Lyf add mastery when casting, but the level-up check ignores them. They only let you cast one power higher without failing.

Ninja

Throwing

A throw earns 8 for anything that is not a weapon, 12 for a weapon, and 12 + a quarter of its throw damage for most weapons. That includes swords, maces, clubs, daggers and axes, not only the throwing weapons. Weight never enters it. Weight only costs stamina, and heavy things fly a shorter distance. Using THROW from the attack menu instead of the cursor adds +5 to the same skill. Each throw makes the champion busy for 4 ticks.

Hand-to-hand

An attack with no monster in the square is a miss, and a miss halves both the experience and the busy time. A wall counts the same as empty air, unless it's a closed door.

Recipe

Clicks are the real limit, not busy time. Each attack takes two clicks, and the game handles one click per tick for the whole party. With champions taking turns, a busy time of up to about 6 ticks costs nothing. Kick a closed door that can't be broken, with a YA potion to hand for stamina. For the Throw skill, throw slayers at the wall in front of you.

Fighters too

The door works for the Fighter's attacks as well, all at full experience and a busy time of 6: BERZERK 40 (and 26 stamina or more), BASH 11, CHOP 10, HACK 9, SWING 6.

Priest

Casting has no busy time, so mana is the only limit, and the measure that counts is experience per point of mana. A spell's experience grows with its difficulty, D = the spell's required skill + power (LO = 1 … MON = 6):

exp = random(0–7) + 16·D + 8·(power − 1)·required + D²

Recipe

YA, at the highest power you can cast without risk. Exp per mana hardly changes with power, so a higher power mostly saves casts and flasks. What costs you is failing. For each mastery level you are short of required + power, the game makes a wisdom test. Each is passed with chance (min(Wisdom + 15, 115) + 1) / 128. A failure burns the mana, makes no potion, and halves the experience once for every missing level. Each potion needs an empty flask in hand: cast, drink (or hand it to a fighter), cast again.

Recipe

FUL while the wizard is young. It's harmless, works anywhere, and earns more per mana at higher power. Move to OH VEN at mastery 4, but cast it down a long corridor. Cast at a wall in front of you, the cloud lands on the party, just as a fireball explodes on it.

What it adds up to

A priest with mastery 8 in Heal stands on level 13 while a monster attacks and casts YA at MON: 276 × 6 × 2 ≈ 3,300 experience for 13 mana. The same cast alone on level 2 earns 276.
