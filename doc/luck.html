Luck in Dungeon Master

The hidden seventh attribute: what it does, what it doesn't, how it gets spent and how it comes back. The Rabbit's Foot is worth less than its reputation says, but it does help.

Read from Paul R. Stevens' CSBWin C++ source · Thresholds read from the Atari graphics.dat · Starting values from dungeon.dat

The short version: Luck is a last chance, not a modifier. It never changes how hard you hit, how hard you're hit, whether you're wounded or whether a spell works. It gives a champion one more roll when everything else has already failed, and only in three places in the whole game.

The roll

The whole mechanic is one routine, IsCharacterLucky(champion, needed). Each caller passes a needed value, and the lower it is, the easier the roll.

Coin flip, and a random 0–99 above needed? ├─ yes → LUCKY Luck isn't read and doesn't change └─ no → random 0 … Luck−1 above needed? ├─ yes → LUCKY, Luck − 2 └─ no → UNLUCKY, Luck + 2 never above the champion's maximum

Two things follow from this.

  • Everybody gets the free chance. The first step ignores Luck completely: half of (99 − needed) in 100. A champion with no Luck at all still gets it.
  • The second step only works above the threshold. If Luck is no higher than needed + 1, no roll can beat it. Below that line, extra Luck does nothing, not even a little.
P(lucky) = ½·(99 − needed)/100 + (rest) · (Luck − 1 − needed)/Luck

The three places it counts

5–57Landing a blow When a champion's attack would miss, a lucky roll makes it land. The threshold depends on the attack.
100−QKeeping your item When a Giggler steals, a lucky roll stops it. Quick champions need less luck.
What Luck never touches
  • Damage you deal
  • Damage you take, once a blow lands
  • The chance of a wound
  • Poison from a blow
  • Spell success and fizzles
  • Missiles, spells and clouds that hit you
  • Traps, pits and doors
  • Experience and level-ups

Dodging a monster's blow

Before a monster rolls any damage, the champion gets two chances to escape the blow completely: no damage, no wound, no poison.

  1. Quickness. If the champion is at least as quick as the monster's dexterity plus a random 0–31 (adjusted by the level's difficulty), the blow misses three times in four.
  2. Luck, with needed = 60.

No champion in the Hall can use their Luck to dodge

The highest starting Luck is WUUF's 60, and the dodge needs 62 before Luck counts at all. For a recruited party, Luck makes no difference to the blows they take. Every champion gets the same flat 19.5 %, from the step that doesn't read Luck. A Rabbit's Foot only starts to matter on someone who already has 52 Luck or more.

Landing a blow

A champion's physical attack first compares their Quickness against the monster's dexterity plus a random 0–31. If that fails, the blow gets two more chances: a flat 1 in 4 (CSBWin names it luckyHit, but it doesn't read Luck), then the luck roll. If all three fail, the blow misses, costs 2–3 stamina, and earns half the experience.

Here, needed is 75 minus a per-attack value from graphics.dat, so some attacks need far less luck than others. Quick, precise attacks are the easiest to rescue.

On a fast weapon, Luck almost makes the Quickness check irrelevant: a JAB with Luck 40 lands 1/4 + 3/4 × 92 % ≈ 94 % of the times Quickness says it misses. On a heavy one it's a modest bonus.

Keeping your item from a Giggler

A Giggler's blow doesn't hurt. It steals instead. The game makes up to five tries, starting with needed = 100 − Quickness and lowering it by 20 each try, and the theft ends at the first lucky roll. Because of a quirk in the original code, every try looks at the ready hand, so whatever is held there goes on the first unlucky roll, and the backpack is never touched.

In practice

Your ready-hand item survives only if the first roll is lucky, or if the champion's Quickness is 100 or more. With Quickness 40 (needed 60), that's the same 19.5 % as the dodge for anyone below 62 Luck. Quickness helps more than Luck here.

Spending it and getting it back

The second step of the roll pays for itself. Each time it saves you, Luck goes down 2. Each time it fails, Luck goes up 2, but never above the champion's maximum. A long fight against something that keeps hitting wears Luck down from its top.

Luck also drifts back to its maximum, like every other attribute. Below the maximum it gains 1 every 256 ticks, about once a minute, or every 64 ticks while the party sleeps. Above the maximum it loses a little at the same rhythm.

The maximum is what you actually build:

What to do with it

Give the Rabbit's Foot to your luckiest front-rank fighter, ideally someone at 52 or more, so the foot lifts them past 61 and their Luck starts working on dodges. Keep cursed items off that champion. Expect only a modest gain. Luck is a small safety net, and Quickness does the real dodging and hitting.
